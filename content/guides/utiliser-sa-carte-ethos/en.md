# Explain why someone should choose you, without overpromising

“I help businesses accelerate growth.” The outcome sounds valuable, but the sentence does not explain your work or the evidence behind your promise. Start with a real project rather than an adjective. You can do this exercise using your notes or an Ethos Card.

In this fictional example, Camille is an independent designer. A three-person team asked her to review its online sign-up journey. She watched five people try the form, noted where they became confused, and proposed a shorter prototype. The team chose that prototype. No post-launch conversion data is available.

Camille can describe what she observed, designed and delivered. She cannot yet claim that she increased sales. The team's decision establishes that the prototype was selected; it does not demonstrate commercial impact.

An accurate introduction would be:

> I am an independent designer. On one project for a three-person team, I watched five people try its sign-up form, noted where they became confused and proposed a shorter prototype. The team selected it; its effect on conversion remains unknown.

Build your own version from one episode:

| Question | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| Who faced which difficulty? | A specific person or team in a specific situation. |
| What did you personally do? | Your actions, distinguished from the team's work. |
| What changed? | An observed outcome, or just the deliverable if its effect is unknown. |
| What evidence can you show? | An authorised excerpt, a contextualised measurement or a documented decision. |
| What can you not claim yet? | A limitation, missing information or an unmeasured outcome. |

If you are starting out, use a voluntary, personal or training project and label it accordingly. It may demonstrate a skill; it does not establish that a customer will pay for it.

**Your next step:** show your introduction to someone who might face this problem and ask, “In what situation would you call on me?” If their answer does not match the work you want to do, revise the words that caused confusion. A compliment about your writing is not validation of your offer.

Before publishing evidence, check that you have permission to share it. Removing a client's name is insufficient if other details still identify them without their consent.

## Use Your Card

[Ethos: why choose you?](/en/cartographies/ethos) guides this exploration using
your own situations. The Card and proposed bio in the Report are statements to
check. The axes describe support in your account; they do not grade your worth
or verify the records you describe.

Keep the formulation your facts support, note its limit and take the next step
before expanding the promise. Camille's case is a fictional editorial example,
not a measured model output or a customer result.
